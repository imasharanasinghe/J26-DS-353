## RQ4b. Does the progression policy raise demonstrated Bloom level across sessions?
- 300 simulated learners (equal numbers of novice, intermediate, advanced), 8 sessions, seed 2026, target concept `heap`.
- Conditions: **A** unverified; **B** current C4; **C** progressive C4; ablations **C_no_fading** (generic support) and **C_no_stretch** (never above the demonstrated level).
- Outcome: capability (Bloom levels as a real number) gained from the first to the last session. Paired differences have a seeded bootstrap 95% interval, a Wilcoxon signed-rank p-value (normal approximation) and Cohen's dz.

### Assumptions
- Learner capability is a real number; the true Bloom level is floor(capability + 0.5).
- ZPD model: learning is fastest when the explanation is above capability but within reach (1 level, plus 0.5 / 0.25 / 0 for full / partial / minimal support); too easy gives little; too hard gives none.
- Flat control: learning is independent of the explanation level.
- Matched control: learning is fastest when the explanation is at the learner's level; stretching hurts.
- Learning is halved while the target's prerequisites are not mastered.
- Condition A draws the explanation level uniformly from 1-4; B caps that draw at the static ceiling.
- Conditions A and B receive generic partial support; C receives full, partial or minimal support by policy.
- Check answers are Bernoulli with logistic probability in (capability + support - question level).
- Parameters are pilot values chosen for plausibility, not fitted to data from real learners.

**Caveat.** Simulated learners are not students. Under the ZPD model the advantage of condition C follows partly from the model's own assumption; the flat and matched controls show where the mechanism does not help. The result supports the design and its limits, not a claim about real learners. The human pilot (docs/PILOT_STUDY.md) is the validation.

### Reading the results honestly
- The stretch is what helps. The ablation without it loses most of the gain, while removing the fading gives no gain or loss beyond noise in this learner model (it has no dependence effect for fading to protect).
- Progression does not speed up reaching level 3; it raises the level reached later, and the benefit needs enough sessions (about zero at 4, clear at 8, larger at 12).
- When stretching does not help learners (matched control) the mechanism ties at best and is slower to level 3; under 12 sessions it is worse.
- The policy starts every unseen concept at Remember, so learners who start high lose early sessions; a placement check would address this.
- The observed Bloom level lags or leads the true level (tracking error above), which limits how well the policy can aim.
